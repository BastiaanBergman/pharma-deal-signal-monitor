# Pharma BD Signal Glossary

Reference for interpreting deal-relevant language in pharmaceutical press releases.
Read relevant sections when a press release passage contains unfamiliar terminology
or when the user asks for clarification on what a term implies for BD activity.

## 1. Deal structures

**License agreement**
One party (licensor) grants another (licensee) the right to develop and/or
commercialise an asset in exchange for financial consideration. The licensor
retains ownership; the licensee gets use rights. The most common BD transaction
in pharma.

**Option agreement**
The potential licensee pays a smaller upfront fee for the right to *decide later*
whether to take a full license, typically after seeing Phase I or Phase II data.
A company announcing an option agreement has not yet transacted a full deal —
the real deal signal is when the option is exercised or allowed to lapse.

**Co-development agreement**
Both parties share the cost and responsibility of clinical development, typically
in exchange for shared commercial rights. Often signals that neither party has
the full capability or capital to develop alone. Look for cost-sharing language
("shared development costs", "joint steering committee").

**Co-promotion / co-commercialisation agreement**
The asset is already approved or near approval; this is a commercial-stage deal
to share sales force and marketing. Not a pipeline signal — indicates the asset
is further along than typical BD targets.

**Merger / acquisition (M&A)**
Full ownership transfer. Distinct from licensing. A company being acquired is
no longer an independent BD counterpart. A company making an acquisition may
be signalling it prefers to buy rather than license — relevant context for
understanding their appetite.

**Asset purchase**
A company buys a specific programme outright rather than the whole company.
Distinct from M&A in that only the asset (IP, data, clinical material) transfers,
not the broader entity.

**Collaboration agreement**
Broad term covering many structures. Requires reading the specifics to determine
whether it is a license, co-development, research collaboration, or discovery
partnership. Research collaborations (pre-IND) are weaker BD signals than
clinical-stage deals.

**ROFR / ROFO (Right of First Refusal / Right of First Offer)**
A large partner has a contractual right to be offered the asset before it goes
to market. A company announcing expiry or termination of an ROFR/ROFO is
effectively signalling the asset is now available more broadly.

## 2. Rights and territory language

**Global rights**
The licensee controls development and commercialisation worldwide. Strongest
deal structure from the licensee's perspective; suggests the licensor traded
territory for a stronger upfront or partner.

**Ex-US rights / ex-North America rights**
The licensor retains US (or North American) rights and partners out the rest
of the world. Common when a smaller company wants to self-commercialise in the
US but lacks international infrastructure. Signals the US asset may still be
available.

**Asia-Pacific (APAC) rights / Greater China rights**
Increasingly common deal structure where Chinese or pan-Asian rights are carved
out separately. A company announcing an APAC deal for an asset *does not* mean
the US or EU rights are taken — those may still be available.

**Retained rights**
Language like "retaining [territory/indication] rights" in an announcement
explicitly signals what is *not* in the deal and may still be available.

**Sublicensing rights**
Whether the licensee can further license to third parties. Relevant for
understanding deal complexity but not a direct signal.

## 3. Financial terms

**Upfront payment**
Cash paid at deal signing, before any milestones are hit. In press releases,
often described as "upfront payment of $X million". Size is relative to asset
stage — a $5M upfront for a Phase I asset is very different from $5M for
Phase III.

**Milestone payments**
Contingent payments triggered by specific events: regulatory (IND clearance,
NDA submission, approval), clinical (Phase I completion, Phase II readout,
Phase III initiation), or commercial (first sale, revenue thresholds). Total
deal value figures in press releases almost always include all milestones,
making them highly speculative — a "$500M deal" may have $20M guaranteed.

**Royalties**
Percentage of net sales paid to the licensor once the product is commercialised.
Rarely disclosed precisely in press releases; language like "tiered royalties
on net sales" is typical. Royalty rates vary widely by asset stage, therapeutic
area, and deal structure.

**Equity investment**
A partner takes a stake in the company alongside or instead of upfront cash.
Common in early-stage deals. Signals strategic alignment but also that the
licensor may not have had leverage to demand a large cash upfront.

**Non-dilutive funding**
Capital raised that does not issue new shares — grants, government contracts,
royalty monetisation. A signal that the company is extending runway without
giving up equity, often while preserving the asset for a future licensing deal.

## 4. Clinical and regulatory milestones

**IND (Investigational New Drug) filing / clearance**
Regulatory submission (US) that allows a company to begin human trials. IND
clearance means the FDA has not objected within 30 days. Signals the asset is
entering clinical stage and entering the zone where many licensees begin
serious evaluation. IND filings in other markets: CTA (Clinical Trial
Authorisation) in EU, IMPD in UK.

**Phase I**
First-in-human studies, primarily focused on safety and dose-finding. Small
patient numbers. At this stage, efficacy data is limited; deal interest is
typically based on mechanism and preclinical package.

**Phase II**
Proof-of-concept studies. The most active BD deal stage — enough efficacy
signal to justify larger investment, enough risk remaining to make a licensing
deal attractive to both sides. Positive Phase II data is the single strongest
pre-partnering signal.

**Phase III**
Pivotal studies powering regulatory submissions. Deals here are larger and
rarer — the asset is substantially de-risked, so the licensor can often demand
premium terms or self-commercialise.

**Primary endpoint met**
The study achieved its pre-specified main efficacy measure. The most significant
positive clinical signal. Often immediately precedes partnering activity.

**Statistically significant**
The result is unlikely to be due to chance (typically p<0.05). Required language
for a credible efficacy claim. Absence of this phrase in a readout should be noted.

**Overall survival (OS) benefit**
In oncology, improvement in how long patients live. The gold standard endpoint
for most tumour types. An OS benefit is a stronger licensing signal than
progression-free survival (PFS) alone.

**PDUFA date**
The FDA's target date for completing review of an NDA/BLA. A named PDUFA date
means approval is imminent. Companies at this stage are more likely seeking
commercial partners than development partners.

**Breakthrough Therapy Designation (BTD)**
FDA designation for drugs showing substantial improvement over existing therapies
in early evidence. Strong de-risking signal; often precedes licensing activity
as it accelerates FDA engagement.

**Fast Track Designation**
FDA designation for drugs addressing serious conditions with unmet need. More
common than BTD; meaningful but less selective.

**Orphan Drug Designation (ODD)**
Granted for diseases affecting fewer than 200,000 patients in the US. Comes
with development incentives (7-year market exclusivity, tax credits). Common
in rare disease BD; signals the company is building the regulatory package.

**Complete Response Letter (CRL)**
FDA rejection of an NDA/BLA with requests for additional data. A significant
setback. May signal the company needs a partner's resources to address the
FDA's concerns, or may cause existing partners to reconsider.

## 5. Platform vs. asset signals

**Platform technology**
A discovery engine or modality that can generate multiple assets (e.g. a
protein degradation platform, an mRNA delivery system, an antibody engineering
platform). Platform deals are typically structured as research collaborations
with option rights over resulting programmes — different BD motion than
single-asset licensing.

**Lead programme / lead asset**
The most advanced or most prioritised asset in a company's pipeline. A deal
on the lead programme often includes rights or options over follow-on assets
in the same platform or indication cluster.

**Pipeline prioritisation**
Language indicating a company is narrowing focus — "prioritising [asset] in
[indication]" often implies other programmes may be available for out-licensing
or deprioritised entirely.

## 6. Company stage signals

**Biotech / emerging biotech**
Typically pre-revenue, single- or few-asset companies. Primary BD motion is
out-licensing or co-development. Most active deal counterparts for large pharma
in-licensing.

**Specialty pharma**
Smaller commercial-stage companies focused on a specific therapeutic area.
May be in-licensing to build a portfolio or out-licensing non-core assets.

**Virtual / lean biotech**
No internal lab infrastructure; relies on CROs and CMOs. Often signals limited
capital and urgency to partner before a financing gap.

**SPAC / reverse merger**
A company that recently went public via a special purpose acquisition company.
Often carries high cash burn and pressure to demonstrate pipeline value quickly
— may signal near-term partnering urgency.

## 7. Language that is NOT a deal signal

These phrases appear frequently and can be mistaken for signals. Treat with caution:

**"Exploring strategic options"** without further context — this is standard
investor relations boilerplate and appears in nearly every annual report.
Only meaningful if paired with specific asset or territory language.

**Research collaboration with an academic institution** — these are typically
not commercial licensing deals. They establish early access and publication
rights, not development or commercialisation rights.

**Manufacturing / CMO agreement** — a contract with a contract manufacturer
is an operational deal, not a BD signal. It may indicate scale-up ahead of
a deal, but is not a deal itself.

**"We continue to evaluate partnership opportunities"** — universal language
in investor presentations. Treat as baseline noise unless paired with something
more specific.

**Poster / abstract at a conference** — data presentation alone is not a signal.
It is pre-signal: companies present data to attract partner interest. Watch for
what follows the presentation, not the presentation itself.
